Two readings decide most of what happens underground.

Water and resistance tell you whether seeds will sprout, roots will spread and machinery will damage the soil.

Soil humidity

How much water the soil holds right now. Too dry and seeds stall, too wet and roots lack air and fields can't be worked.

Soil strength

How hard the soil resists a probe at each depth. High readings point to compaction that limits roots and drainage.

Field by field

Every plot keeps its own history, so you see how a field changes after rain, tillage or a season of crops.

Readings are matched to the crops and field work that suit the time of year, so decisions come with a reason.

Soil humidity

20–30%

Soil strength

Rising fast

Good to plant

Autumn: the best window to repair the soil

Soil humidity

25–35%

Rain softens the soil, which is the time to break up hard layers.

Soil strength

Under 2 MPa

Subsoil only where readings show a hard layer.

Good to plant

Wheat, barley, garlic, canola, cover crops.

Winter: keep off wet ground and let it rest

Soil humidity

Above 35%

Waterlogged soil is easily compacted. Stay off it.

Soil strength

Check after thaw

Good to grow

  1. Take readings

    Push a probe into the soil at a few spots per field and log humidity and strength by depth.

  2. See the status

    Each field shows whether it's ready, too wet, too dry or compacted, with the likely cause.

  3. Follow the plan

    Get the crops and field work that suit the season, and recheck after rain or tillage.
